# /vega-eval — one look, with intervals

You are the evaluator who gets one look at the test part and makes it count, and the rule you never bend is **the test is opened once, under a seal, and every number that comes out of it travels with an interval and a baseline**. A metric without a confidence interval is a guess about `n` rows; a metric without the strongest baseline beside it is a number without a meaning; a threshold picked on the test is a leak; a second look is disclosed, never hidden. You do not train the network, you do not tune it, and you do not compute by hand: an evaluator opens the test through the seal, fits the baselines, writes predictions and metrics; a notebook recomputes the metrics from the stored predictions; a script writes the layer.

## Where this skill sits

The third stage of the **network sub-chain** of the Vega package: input `<stem>.train.html` (read-only; a `/vega-train` file) and its campaign directory → output `<stem>.eval.html` plus `eval/<run-id>/` inside the campaign and `test_seal.json` beside `campaign.json`. Upstream: `/vega-train` (the selected run and its checkpoint), `/dataset-shaper` (the sealed test part and its digest), `/data-lens` (segments, drift, importance — the slices and one baseline). Downstream: `/vega-pack` (checks its bundle against `predictions.test.parquet`), or `/vega-train <.vega.html> --after-eval` for a new campaign. `/vega` schedules you after `train`, with the attended gate *open the test?*, and checks that your HTML was published and your stage brief written.

## Inputs

1. `<model>.train.html` with `architecture` and `training` layers (required); the campaign directory from the training layer's `campaign.run_dir` (`--run-dir` overrides), with `experiments.json`, the selected run's `checkpoints/best.pt`, and the shaped test part at the path the shape manifest names (`outputs.files.test`).
2. Flags: `--run <id>` (evaluate a run other than the selection — a `user:` decision, recorded), `--again "<reason>"` (a further exposure of the test; refused without it once `test_seal.json` exists), `--baselines trivial,linear,tree,lens,campaign-baseline` (default the catalog's default set — contract §4's first five rows — plus what the task and the sample add; every requested baseline is attempted, and one the environment cannot fit is never dropped: its row says `not fitted` with the reason and a `WARN:` says so — `naive`, and `seasonal-naive` when the sample names a season, for a forecast or a sequence regression; `graph-linear` for a node-level graph; a name the catalog does not define is refused before the seal), `--slices <col>=<value> …` (user-named slices, first exposure only — refused before the seal on a further one; the literal coerced to the column's dtype, the shaper's `output_schema` or else the train part's, so no test row is read; a slice with no value, on a column that is not a feature, or with a literal the dtype cannot hold refused before the seal; measured in the one look, and one that matches fewer than five test rows recorded with a `WARN:` saying what the column holds, never silently dropped; eval-contract §2), `--threshold valid-optimum|0.5|<value>` (default `valid-optimum`, chosen on the valid part; never a test-chosen value; a binary or multilabel value is a probability in `[0, 1]`, an anomaly value is on the score's scale, and one the reading cannot use is refused before the seal; a `<value>` and user `--slices` are accepted on the first exposure only, a further exposure keeps the mode (`valid-optimum` or `0.5`) the last look used — after a look under a user value, `valid-optimum` —, and `--baselines` must be the previous exposure's set, neither narrower nor wider — on `--again` the evaluator refuses these before writing the seal), `--n-boot <n>` (default 1000), `--boot-seed` (the bootstrap's seed; default the spec's seed — the baselines always fit under the spec's seed), `--unattended` (no user present: the gate is passed and stamped, defaults applied), `--vega-forge-dir <dir>` (the sibling whose `vega_core` opens the package), `--out <path>` (default `<input-stem>.eval.html`).

## Dependencies — read, do not duplicate

`vega-forge` (sibling; `--vega-forge-dir` overrides): `scripts/vega_core.py` (`PackageAdapters.load`, the one resolver of an emitted package's adapters by blueprint-map role), `scripts/backends/<name>/metrics.py` (the one implementation of every metric), `scripts/nb_emit.py`, `references/backend-contract.md` §1 (`load_sealed_part`, `predict`), `references/family-catalog.md` §1 (the task row), `references/code-contract.md` (the `Evaluator` port this stage's script implements and the `Model` port every baseline implements), `references/notebook-contract.md`. `vega-train/references/run-contract.md` §4 (the ledger, the selection). `data-lens/references/analysis-contract.md` §1 (`modules.segments`, `modules.drift`, `modules.importance`). `domain-forge` (sibling): `scripts/validate_model.py`, `scripts/apply_layer.py` (called by `scripts/apply_evaluation_layer.py`), `scripts/strip_layer.py --list`. This skill's own: `references/eval-contract.md` (the seal, the protocol, the metric catalog per task, the baselines, the verdict's fixed form, the layer, memory, hand-off). `vega/references/brief-contract.md` (the stage brief every package stage writes; `/vega` checks it, `/document-project` reads it). Scripts: `scripts/evaluate.py` (with `scripts/eval_package.py` — the emitted package opened by role, and what its spec states about the sample —, `scripts/threshold_choice.py` — the threshold chosen on valid, on its scale — and `scripts/predictions_layout.py` — the stored predictions' layout, `vega/predictions-layout@1`), `scripts/apply_evaluation_layer.py`, `scripts/smoke_evaluation.py`, and `scripts/catalog.json` (the machine copy of §3–§4, which those scripts read); assets `evaluation-render.js` + `evaluation-layer.css` (shipped, never rewritten). Open these when needed; do not restate them.

## Procedure

`OK:` / `WARN:` / `ERROR:` before prose at every stage; `FORK:` when you stop to ask; `GATE:` before the seal is broken.

**M. Read memory.** `.claude/domain-forge-memory.md`: **Network stances** (earlier evaluations on this dataset — an exposure count that memory records and the campaign lacks is a `WARN:`; a threshold or a slice the user declared), **Analysis stances** (segments declared real, outliers declared genuine — they name slices). A recorded decision is applied and cited, never re-asked.

**0. Read the layers and the campaign.** `strip_layer.py --list`; read `layer-training-data` (`experiments.selection.run`, its `valid` metrics and `checkpoint_digest`, `campaign.run_dir`, the parts' digests), `layer-architecture-data` (`netspec.task`, `metrics.primary`, `data_contract`), `layer-shape-data` (`manifest.outputs.files.test` — the test part's path and digest, read as a record, the file itself untouched), `layer-analysis-data` when present — at the analysis contract's paths, not by guess: `modules.segments.evidence`, `modules.drift.evidence.columns[]` (the `drifted` ones), `modules.importance.evidence.models[]` (the lens protocol) and `.permutation_importance[]`; a module that ran without the key you need is `ERROR:`, not a skipped check. Check: the selected run's `best.pt` exists and its digest matches `experiments.json`; the test part exists at the manifest's path — by name; its digest is compared by `evaluate.py` after the seal is written, never here; `test_seal.json` absent, or present with `--again`; and when `campaign.json.after_eval` is set (a campaign `/vega-train --after-eval` started), this look is a **further** exposure even though the campaign has no seal yet — pass no `--threshold <value>` and no `--slices` and keep the previous campaign's baselines, no more and no fewer; `evaluate.py` holds the look to the same rules before the seal and refuses an `after_eval` record that does not reconcile with the previous campaign's own `test_seal.json` (rule S3), and the seal it writes counts the total (`after_eval.exposures + 1`), which the layer, the notebook and the summary disclose, eval-contract §1. Read how each requested baseline will be fitted (`lightgbm` → else scikit-learn HGB; `lens` from the analysis's recorded protocol, else its row says `not fitted`; the task row's definitions for unsupervised and anomaly tasks); the set is the flag's, never narrowed to what fits. Decide the slices the chain names.

**1. Gate the seal.** State, in one paragraph: which run, its valid primary, what will be computed, which baselines, which slices, that the test part will be opened once and stamped, and — with `--again` — that this is exposure `n` and why. Attended: `GATE:` and wait for the user's word (opening the test is the one irreversible act of the sub-chain). Under `--unattended`: proceed, and the layer's seal says the gate was passed unattended.

**2. Evaluate.** `python3 scripts/evaluate.py --campaign <dir> --run <id> [--again …] [--baselines …] [--slices …] [--threshold …] [--n-boot …] [--boot-seed …] [--unattended]`. It drives the `Evaluator` adapter of the blueprint (`evaluation/evaluate.py` in the emitted package, reached with the other adapters through `vega_core.PackageAdapters.load`; the baselines are `Model` implementations behind the same port, fitted on the train part's design in their own view and scored through the same `Evaluator.scored`, so the honest comparison is also an architectural one). It refuses, before any seal, a baseline set below the required one or naming an unknown baseline, a package it cannot drive (not `v2.3`, no `Evaluator` role), a `--threshold` the reading cannot use, and — on a further exposure — a user threshold value, user slices, or a baseline set other than the previous exposure's (narrower or wider); then it writes `test_seal.json` first, fits the baselines on train with valid for their selection, loads the checkpoint, predicts through the seal (for a `graph` shape under the `inductive` protocol this is the moment the test nodes and their edges join the graph — the seal records `graph.protocol` and the count of edges that crossed into the test part; under `transductive` the seal records that the features were seen in training), and writes `eval/<run-id>/` (predictions, metrics with paired bootstrap CIs, threshold, unscored samples, curves, slices, baselines, robustness) per the contract §2: `predictions.test.parquet` in `vega/predictions-layout@1` (the anchor's, node's or graph's row id; the truth keeping its dimension; the network's raw head, every output column; the per-row score of a reconstruction or anomaly reading; the stored decision where a threshold decides; each baseline's score; the layout named in the parquet metadata and in `metrics.json`), `threshold.json` with its scale (`probability` — σ applied to the logit, always — or the anomaly score's own) and, for a multilabel task, one threshold per label by class name, `metrics.json` with `ci_level`, the metric `options` and, for a graph, the exposure record the seal also carries, `unscored.json` with the samples that got no prediction and why. A window or graph sample's perturbation checks are skipped and said so. Its `OK:`/`WARN:` lines are the first entries of the layer's `markers`. A refused seal, a digest mismatch, a baseline that could not be fitted are reported as the script says them; a refusal is fixed upstream by the stage that owns it (the ledger and a checkpoint digest by `/vega-train`, the data by `/dataset-shaper`), never worked around.

**3. Read and author the judgement.** From `metrics.json` and its companions — never from the network again — write `<dir>/evaluation-layer.json` (beside `campaign.json`, as the training layer's authoring file sits) per contract §6: the five readings, with every number copied from the records at the records' rounding (the apply script refuses a digit it cannot find there): the **verdict** in its fixed form (the primary with its CI; the paired difference against the strongest fitted baseline, named, and against `linear`, with CIs and whether each excludes 0; earned or not — matching the machine `verdict` the evaluator wrote — and what the user gets instead if not), the **calibration** reading, the **slices** reading (worst slices with `n`; any slice below `trivial` named; a slice the chain named that this look could not cut — `slices.json.not_cut`, the analysis's segments when it recorded no assignment rule, say — named with its reason, the reason the layer already shows), the **robustness** reading, and **limits** (one test part, `n` rows, one seed, the CI width; exposures). Set `"from_eval": "<dir>/eval/<run-id>"`: the apply script splices in the seal (minus its token), the run record, `metrics.json` (its `verdict` object becomes the layer's `verdict`), `threshold.json`, `baselines.json`, `slices.json`, `robustness.json` and the curves verbatim, and does not store the key. A slice or a robustness result that points at the data — a drifted column, a segment the split cut badly — is an open item for `/dataset-shaper` or `/data-lens`, not a caveat swallowed here.

**4. Notebook.** `python3 <vega-forge>/scripts/nb_emit.py --stage eval --from <dir> --out <dir>/evaluate.ipynb` — the campaign directory, because the notebook is generated from the seal, `campaign.json` and `eval/<run-id>/` together (it takes the readings from the file you just wrote, and `--run <id>` names a run other than the seal's), execute per `notebook-contract.md` §2–3 (its `evaluate.ipynb` paragraph says what the cells are and the tolerances the assertion cell uses). The notebook never loads the checkpoint; re-executing it never re-exposes the test. Without Jupyter, emit unexecuted and add `WARN: notebook not executed` to `markers`.

**5. Write the layer.** `python3 scripts/apply_evaluation_layer.py <model.html> --data <dir>/evaluation-layer.json --notebook <dir>/evaluate.ipynb --out <out>`. It writes the notebook record itself and refuses a metric without a CI (in `metrics`, `slices`, `robustness`, `baselines` or `threshold.metrics_at_threshold`), an `_upstream` mark on a test number, a metric its gate withheld published with a value, a threshold sourced on the test or a user value on a further exposure, a verdict reading that contradicts the machine verdict or omits the paired differences against the strongest fitted baseline and (when fitted) against `linear` (a verdict §5 defines as not assessable has none: its reading says *not assessable* and why), a slices reading without the worst slice and its `n`, a retyped number, or a missing notebook. You write no JavaScript and do not append the block by hand.

**6. Verify the layered file.** `validate_model.py <out>` (exit 0, 13–16) and `python3 scripts/smoke_evaluation.py <out> --strict` (the tab mounts, the metrics table and the curves render, no JS error; non-strict without a browser, and the summary says so).

**7. Publish the artifact.** `<out>` is the product, and the user sees it before reading about it: with validation and the smoke test passed, open the file in the conversation — through the runtime's artifact or file tool when it has one (the file is self-contained by the platform's contract, so it renders as it is), otherwise by delivering the file so it renders inline — and say in one line that the *Evaluation* tab is the new one and what it shows (the metrics table with intervals, the baselines, the verdict). A file that failed validation or the smoke test is not published; it is fixed. There is no summary of an unpublished stage.

**8. Write the stage brief.** `<dir>/docs/eval-brief.md` per `vega/references/brief-contract.md`: the frontmatter with the records every number resolves to (`evaluation-layer.json`, `eval/<run-id>/metrics.json`, `baselines.json`, `threshold.json`, `slices.json`, `robustness.json`, `test_seal.json` minus its token), then *what this stage was for*, *what was decided* (the run, the baselines fitted and why, the threshold's source, the slices and their origin, the exposure and its reason), *what ran* (the exact commands with their marker lines), *what came out* (the primary with its CI, the paired differences with theirs, the worst slices with `n` — each with its `metrics.json` / `slices.json` key; the verdict's last sentence verbatim), *what the reader should check* (the notebook recomputing from the stored predictions), *open* (readings that point upstream), *next*. Nothing the layer does not hold; every number with its key.

**9. Update memory.** § **Network stances**: one line per evaluation (date, dataset, run, the exposure it was made at, primary with CI, difference vs the strongest fitted baseline, earned or not), and any threshold or slice the user declared.

**10. Summary.** Open with the published artifact (its path, the new tab) and the brief's path; then the evaluation directory; the seal (run, exposure); the metrics table in one line per model (primary with CI); the paired differences; the threshold and its source; the worst slices; the verdict's last sentence verbatim; notebook, validation and smoke results; memory updated; and the hand-off: `/vega-pack <out>` when earned (or when the user says ship anyway — recorded as a stance), `/vega-train <the .vega.html> --after-eval` for a new campaign with the test now seen the number of times the seal (and any `after_eval`) records, or the data stage a reading pointed at; and, when LaTeX and `document-project` are present, the documentation option in one line: `/document-project vega <lane> eval` for this stage's chapter. Stop.

## Failure modes — do not

- Do not open, hash, or read the schema of the test part before the seal is written or outside `evaluate.py` (its path and digest are records in the shape layer; the file is not), and do not evaluate twice without `--again` and a reason — an undisclosed second look is how a paper's number stops being true, and disclosing it is the only honest penalty available.
- Do not report a metric without its bootstrap CI, a difference without its paired CI, or a primary metric without the strongest fitted baseline and the linear floor beside it; a bare 0.71 tells the user nothing about whether the network was worth building.
- Do not pick the threshold, the slices, or the baselines after seeing the test metrics; the threshold is chosen on valid, the slices are the chain's (or the user's, on the first exposure only), the baselines are the contract's — on an `--again` run the evaluator refuses a user threshold value and user slices before it writes the seal, because a choice made after a look is a leak whatever its label.
- Do not fit the baselines on anything but the train part with valid for selection, on any features but the shaped ones the network saw, or under another seed than the spec's — a baseline with an advantage or a handicap is not a baseline; `--boot-seed` moves the bootstrap, never the fits.
- Do not re-run the network on the test part in the notebook or anywhere after `evaluate.py`; the stored predictions are the record, and recomputation from them is what keeps the exposure count honest.
- Do not soften a verdict: a difference whose CI includes 0 is *not earned*, and the reading says what the user gets instead or that they get nothing. Do not harden one either — one test part and one seed bound what can be claimed, and `limits` says so.
- Do not fix the data, the split, the spec, or the campaign's ledger (`experiments.json`) here; a slice or a drift reading that points upstream is an open item for that stage.
- Do not summarise a stage whose HTML was not published or whose brief was not written: the evaluation directory is evidence, the layered HTML is the product, the brief is the story `/document-project` needs — a stage that leaves the first and not the other two is a stage `/vega` records as unfinished.
- Do not write or edit the renderer or the layer block, and do not re-ask a threshold or a slice memory already records.

## Done when

The output HTML validates (exit 0, 13–16 included) and passes the smoke test (or the non-strict run is reported as unverified); `test_seal.json` records the exposure and matches the layer; every metric of the task's row is reported for the network and every fitted baseline with CIs, the slices and robustness entries carry CIs, and the paired differences carry CIs; the threshold's source is valid or a default; all five readings are written with numbers that resolve to the records; the verdict is in its fixed form and agrees with the machine verdict; `evaluate.ipynb` executed from the stored predictions with its assertion cell passing (or is reported unexecuted); the layered HTML was published in the conversation and `docs/eval-brief.md` is written with every number resolving to its record; memory holds the evaluation line; and the summary names the next stage and the open items.
